# Seasonal community design: a conditional research proposal

**Status: Proposed, research only.** This document recommends a direction for study; it does not accept a privacy policy, authorize collection, or qualify a backend. The Project Steward owns product and data-policy acceptance. Sol owns engineering disposition within those accepted contracts. The source basis is the conversation initiative in [#810](https://github.com/nurockplayer/Jabiko/issues/810), the accepted [Sol plan](https://github.com/nurockplayer/Jabiko/issues/819#issuecomment-5849152288), the complete [Astra read-only advisory](https://github.com/nurockplayer/Jabiko/issues/819#issuecomment-5849152148), the current [seasonal-content audit](seasonal-conversation-content.md), and the [analytics allowlist](analytics.md). Existing seasonal dates select authored learning scenarios; they do not establish local observance or forecasts.

## Recommendation and value

Research one event-wide, delayed, frozen, coarse summary of **submitted completion reports**, alongside a separately optional Japanese memory saved only on the learner’s device. Do not recommend public per-person template cards or public free text for an initial version. This model could provide a weak collective activity signal without a social graph. It cannot show that distinct people participated, that another person is online, or that a reported session was independently verified. A client report is an action report, not proof of attendance or identity.

The learning value must stand on its own when nobody contributes or the shared service is unavailable. A learner can still practise Japanese, save a private draft if they choose, and revisit it next year. That is useful solo learning, not evidence of community participation. Use copy such as “No shared summary is available for this edition.” Never infer that nobody participated, invent peers or counts, or show curated examples as if a learner submitted them. Decorative seasonal art may remain static and clearly decorative; it must not imply a crowd.

This recommendation is conditional NO-GO for shared collection and publication until source integrity, provider metadata, retention/deletion, abuse operations, release inference, and unknown-age boundaries have separate evidence and acceptance. If research finds value depends on live or individualized peer presence, this proposal does not meet that need; do not add hidden identity collection to simulate it.

## Common planning assumptions and workload comparison

The figures below are comparable planning inputs, not observed Jabiko traffic, moderation rates, staffing commitments, or vendor prices. Model one configured event edition in three launched interface languages. Let **N = 100** (low illustration) or **N = 1,000** (high illustration) submitted actions/items. N counts reports or submissions, never people. For incident planning only, assume 1–5% of N produces a report or abuse signal, each needing a 10-minute human triage. That yields 1–5 tickets / 10–50 minutes at N=100 and 10–50 tickets / 100–500 minutes (1.7–8.3 hours) at N=1,000. These rates and handling times are deliberately hypothetical and must be replaced by pilot evidence. Free-text review assumes two minutes per item before publication, also hypothetical. For bounded templates, compare six authored choices across the three launched locales.

All three networked approaches need an accountable operator, access review, incident response, and a kill switch. The solo alternative adds no shared ingestion or publication: budget three localized prompts per edition and test local save, delete, export, and expiry behavior. It offers no observable shared-presence evidence and should be judged on solo learning value, not renamed as community success.

## Proposed data and lifecycle boundaries

If a future pilot is accepted, begin with one separately configured event edition, one bounded collection interval, one fixed post-close release time, and no more than one frozen coarse result. No live counter, threshold progress, exact total, “you plus N others,” personal contribution delta, or repeated cumulative release. Do not split by template, locale, region, age, device, language level, phase, or time of day. Delayed broad bands and suppression mitigate exposure but do not prove anonymity. Repeated releases can be differenced; a sparse observer may know everyone except one, and one submitter may supply most reports. Do not invent a “safe k” or call a threshold k-anonymous. Define attacker knowledge, contribution bounds, and residual inference risk before choosing any release rule.

**Ingress, analytics, and integrity.** Candidate request content is an allowlisted edition ID and fixed action code only. Do not send wishes, memory, selected template, hanami status, exact visit date, location, account ID, response score, learner text, or raw user identifiers. This document authorizes no new GA4/Zaraz event and no repurposing of existing practice analytics as a community database. Keep operational receipts separate from analytics. A one-time receipt might support retry idempotency or scoped deletion, but creates bearer-token and retention obligations. Retry idempotency, limiting repeat actions on one browser, and resisting many fabricated actions are three different properties. Browser markers do not establish unique people; IP limits can burden shared networks; IP hashing remains potentially identifying/linkable. No fingerprint, permanent device ID, account linkage, or hashed-IP scheme is accepted here. If genuine-source handling cannot meet the claim without unacceptable collection, keep shared publication off.

Omitting IP from the application payload does not establish what a CDN, WAF, origin, queue, error reporter, log, backup, or subprocessor records. [Cloudflare documents HTTP request fields](https://developers.cloudflare.com/logs/logpush/logpush-job/datasets/zone/http_requests/); this is available capability, not evidence of Jabiko’s deployed configuration or retention. Inventory and verify actual settings and contracts before making a product promise. Provider feasibility is unknown.

**Retention and choice.** Unsaved Japanese drafts stay local with no background transmission, evaluator, or automatic sync. A possible separately accepted saved-memory policy is device-local storage, explicit save, user delete/export, and a disclosed 13-month expiry so a learner can revisit it next year; this is proposed, not current behavior or a guarantee that a closed browser physically deletes data on schedule. Warn about shared-device access and loss when browser storage is cleared; do not claim encryption, account secrecy, cloud backup, or recoverability. A possible service-record policy would retain reports/receipt linkage only through publication plus a short, fixed correction/deletion window (30 days is a discussion example, not accepted retention), then remove linkage and raw records on a declared schedule. Public output could expire by the next edition, but screenshots and copies cannot be recalled, and deleting a source report cannot ensure that its influence disappears from copied aggregates. Local deletion, service deletion, backup expiry, and public withdrawal are distinct. No retention, export, or deletion guarantee exists by merging this design document.

Treat age as unknown. Do not require age, public original text, profile, contact, precise whereabouts, sensitive disclosure, or attendance. Offer skip and fictional/example practice; keep local memory private by default. Shared-device risks remain. Whether any future collection is permitted for particular ages or jurisdictions needs qualified review; this proposal makes no legal-compliance conclusion.

## Seasonal learning lifecycle

The community layer must serve the curriculum’s Japanese production and Small Talk goals, not reward clicks. A locally saved response is not automatically validated Japanese or evidence of successful practice.

Each candidate follows six distinct stages. **Preview** teaches the cultural context and offers a skip-friendly Japanese example. **Participation** is private conversation practice, not a public post. **Event day** means the configured edition’s practice window, not a claim that a learner attended an event. **POST-CLOSE Recap** happens only after the window closes and a fixed delay; publish at most one qualified frozen coarse result, otherwise say it is unavailable. **Private memory** is optional learner-authored Japanese stored locally and deletable. **Next year** invites the learner to revisit their own saved words, without presuming observance or attendance. The recap never contains public text or status histograms.

**Tanabata — six stages.** **Preview:** introduce the custom of writing a wish, as a bounded learning context rather than a universal practice. **Participation:** practise stating a small wish, giving a reason, then asking and reacting in Japanese; for example, 「今年は毎日少し日本語を読みたいです。短い記事なら続けられそうだからです。あなたは何か続けたいことがありますか？」 This exercises wish-plus-reason and a reciprocal Small Talk question. **Event day:** completing this authored conversation may be followed by one separately chosen completion report; the wish itself stays local. **POST-CLOSE Recap:** use the shared rule above, never show a wish strip or imply that examples came from peers. **Private memory:** save the original wish only on request. **Next year:** optionally compare that private wording with a new wish. July 7 is a learning anchor, not universal observance; some regions hold events around August 7 and local dates vary.

**Setsubun — six stages.** **Preview:** offer a short, reviewed explanation of a custom and make clear that practice does not ask the learner to perform it. **Participation:** respond to one detail, ask a focused follow-up, and react to the answer; for example, 「豆をまく習慣の説明が印象に残りました。どの部分をもっと知りたいですか？」 The job is a brief cultural reaction that opens a Small Talk exchange, not a ritual report. **Event day:** the configured practice window accepts only the ordinary completion action, never bean counts or attendance. **POST-CLOSE Recap:** use the single delayed aggregate or unavailable state, without implying unique celebrants. **Private memory:** optionally save the learner’s own Japanese reaction locally. **Next year:** invite a private reread or revised reaction. Setsubun is not in the current 13-anchor pack; any edition needs separately sourced editorial acceptance. No health-effect claim is proposed.

**Hanami — six stages.** **Preview:** present a seasonal conversation prompt without a date, location, or bloom forecast. **Participation:** let the learner compare an experience, not-yet status, or plan and ask a related question; for example, 「私はまだですが、今年は時間があれば見に行くつもりです。よかったら、今年の予定や気になっていることを聞かせてください。」 The follow-up welcomes a plan or interest without assuming the partner went. This practises sharing an experience or plan and inviting a Small Talk response; alternatives must also work for someone who has not gone. **Event day:** the configured practice window records at most the same completion action, never whether someone went. **POST-CLOSE Recap:** include no visit-status histogram or inferred attendance, only the qualified event-wide result or unavailable state. **Private memory:** an original response may be saved locally by choice. **Next year:** allow a private comparison while keeping “did not go” a valid outcome. A proposed learning window is not a regional bloom forecast.

All three examples retain a Japanese reading or production exercise if no aggregate qualifies.

## Access, fallback, and candidate next research

All journeys need keyboard access, visible focus, screen-reader labels and text equivalents. Do not communicate participation or status by color, animation, or decoration alone; respect reduced-motion settings. Keep Japanese as the learning language and localize interface/help consistently for the three launched UI languages. No new locale launch, social profile, direct message, follower graph, location sharing, public wish feed, open chat, current-event service, automatic forecast, new analytics, or generalized UGC platform is in scope.

Before any implementation proposal, the Steward should accept separately scoped research questions and owners:

1. **Learning/product study (Product):** compare a clearly labelled solo control with a delayed coarse recap; measure task comprehension and whether learners can distinguish action reports from people or live presence. Stop if value depends on individualized/live peers or users infer peer-authored text.
2. **Privacy and sparse-case review (Privacy/Safety):** test all proposed public releases against sparse, correlated, and attacker-dominated contributions, including threshold boundaries and repeated-edition comparison. Stop if the release exposes unacceptable inference; suppression is an honest fallback, not an anonymity certificate.
3. **Integrity and abuse feasibility (Engineering/Operations):** exercise replay, token reset, automated submissions, withdrawal, corruption, and kill-switch procedures with test traffic excluded from public results. Stop if fabricated volume cannot be bounded without unaccepted identity collection.
4. **Provider/lifecycle evidence (Data/Operations):** inventory actual request metadata, logs, backups, subprocessors, deletion/export paths, and retention. Stop if any plane cannot meet a subsequently approved contract.
5. **Age-unknown and accessibility review (Safety/Accessibility):** review skip/fictional alternatives, local-device limitations, keyboard/screen-reader flow, and applicable age handling before any collection or public display.

These are candidate research scopes only, not tickets or authorization to build. Any later pilot needs a named operator, an edition-specific publication/withdrawal policy, reviewed localized copy, and an explicit go/no-go decision. If population is sparse, reports look corrupt, provider retention is unknown, or no operator is available, disable contribution and publication while leaving solo Japanese practice usable. Do not retry or imply a zero-participant result after an unavailable or failed submission.

**Candidate implementation tickets, not created or authorized:** (1) A bounded-ingress and frozen-release service, only after source integrity, metadata, retention, abuse response, sparse-release, and named-operator gates are accepted and demonstrated. (2) An opt-in, accessible seasonal interface for skip-friendly practice and an honest delayed-recap/unavailable state, only after Product and Accessibility accept the exact flow and copy. (3) An opt-in local-memory lifecycle with explicit save, delete/export behavior, shared-device warning, and expiry, only after Data/Privacy accept its storage and retention contract. Each candidate requires Steward acceptance plus the relevant Product, Data/Privacy, Accessibility, and Operations qualifications; none is implementation authority by appearing here.
